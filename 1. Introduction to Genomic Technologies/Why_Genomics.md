# Genomics Overview

## Why Study Genomics
* **Genetic Variation:** Humans are over **99.9%** genetically identical, yet small genomic differences drive diverse traits like height, lifespan, and disease susceptibility.
* **Developmental Programming:** The genome encodes the developmental program from a single cell to a complex organism and determines specialized cell types despite identical DNA in every cell.

---

## Genomics and Disease
* **Cancer Biology:** Cancer is a genetic disease caused by mutations that disrupt normal cell division control, leading to uncontrolled cell growth.
* **Mutation Mechanisms:** Mutations arise from DNA damage or replication errors; studying these alterations helps explain how cancer develops and progresses.

---

## Central Dogma and Information Flow
* **Information Flow:** The central dogma describes the directional flow of genetic information: `DNA` → `RNA` → `Proteins` (which execute cellular functions).
* **Gene Regulation:** Feedback mechanisms allow proteins to regulate gene expression, enabling distinct cell types to emerge from the exact same genome.

---

## Genomic Technologies and Data
* **Sequencing Advancements:** Rapid technological progress has transformed whole-genome sequencing from a massive global effort into a fast, affordable routine process.
* **Computational Genomics:** Massive genomic datasets are publicly available, requiring powerful computing and bioinformatics pipelines to analyze and interpret for research and discovery.
