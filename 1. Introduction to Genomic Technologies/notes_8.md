# Foundational Cell Biology for Genomic Technologies

> **Overview:** This lecture provides a foundational overview of cell biology relevant to understanding modern genomic technologies.

---

## Cell Types and Domains
* **Three Domains of Life:** Cellular organisms are divided into **eukaryotes**, **archaea**, and **bacteria**, with archaea and bacteria collectively grouped as **prokaryotes**.
* **Key Structural Difference:** Eukaryotes possess a membrane-bound nucleus containing their `DNA`, whereas prokaryotes lack a nucleus.

---

## Eukaryotic Cell Structure and DNA Organization
* **Nuclear DNA:** Eukaryotic cells contain a membrane-enclosed nucleus that houses `DNA` organized into chromosomes.
* **Mitochondrial DNA:** Mitochondria—organelles essential for energy metabolism—carry their own small circular `DNA` and likely originated from ancient prokaryotes via endosymbiosis.

---

## Cell Cycle and Division
* **Mitosis:** Cells progress through a cycle culminating in mitosis, where `DNA` replicates and the cell divides into two genetically identical daughter cells, preserving chromosome copy numbers.
* **Diploidy:** Diploid organisms (such as humans) carry two copies of each chromosome—one inherited from each parent.

---

## Specialized Cell Division and Genetic Diversity
* **Stem Cell Differentiation:** Stem cells can differentiate into diverse, specialized cell types through regulated division pathways.
* **Meiosis & Recombination:** Meiosis—the cell division process underlying sexual reproduction—includes chromosomal recombination (*crossing over*), generating genetic diversity among offspring.
